GeoNeural

Terrain representations of a real 10.24 km x 10.24 km region, southern Essen and the Ruhr valley in Germany, compared by the bytes they need, their height error against a 10 m reference grid, and the drainage network a routing algorithm derives from them.

  • No neural codec beats the best conventional one of the same size by a useful margin, and none whose streams were measured keeps more of them.
  • Sparse corrections around streams preserve drainage more cheaply than tightening the whole grid.
  • A learned hillslope closure that predicts one flux per cell face conserves material by construction. A closure trained with a conservation penalty does not reach that balance at any weight.

Rate and age

Can the shape of a landscape tell how fast it formed? Speeding up every process and shortening the time to match leaves one survey unchanged; two dated surveys can tell them apart.

Compression microscope

One region at one error bound, coded three ways: SZ3, a conventional error-bounded codec; cubic-ctx, a multilevel coder with a fixed predictor; and the same multilevel coder with a small learned predictor shared by every field. Every byte of every product is counted, and the learned product is decoded again in your browser from its file.

Where the bytes go

The learned coder needs its model at the decoder. Stored once for a corpus, the model costs less per field the more fields share it; SZ3 and cubic-ctx carry everything in each file.
